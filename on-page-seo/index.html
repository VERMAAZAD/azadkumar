Start with search intent, not just keywords

Ranking for a keyword means little if the page does not give the searcher what they came for. Before touching a page I identify the intent behind the query (informational, commercial, transactional or navigational) by studying what already ranks on Google and Bing. Each page is then built around one clear intent and one primary topic, which avoids keyword cannibalization, where your own pages compete against each other.

The anatomy of a well-optimized page

Title tag

Written for the click: the primary keyword near the front, a clear benefit and a length that avoids truncation.

Meta description

A compelling summary that earns the click. It is not a direct ranking factor, but it affects click-through rate.

Headings

One H1 and logical H2 and H3 headings that mirror how people ask questions and make the page easy to scan.

Body content

Original, complete and genuinely helpful, covering the topic more usefully than competing pages.

Internal links

Descriptive anchor text pointing to related pages, spreading authority and guiding visitors.

Images

Compressed files, descriptive file names and accurate alt text for accessibility and image search.

URL structure

Short, readable, relevant URLs without clutter.

Schema

Matching structured data such as Article, FAQ, Service or Product to clarify the page’s purpose.

How I optimize your pages

  1. Keyword research

    I build a keyword list grouped by topic and intent using Ahrefs, Semrush and Search Console data.

  2. Page mapping

    Each keyword group is assigned to one page: existing, improved or new.

  3. Content gap analysis

    I compare your page with top results to see which questions, subtopics and details are missing.

  4. Optimize and write

    Titles, headings, copy, links and images are updated, or a detailed brief is written for your content team.

  5. Measure

    I track impressions, clicks, positions and conversions in Search Console and GA4, then refine.

Content quality matters more than keyword density

There is no magic keyword percentage. Search engines reward pages that show real experience, accuracy and usefulness. I focus on clear answers, specific examples, up-to-date facts and a structure that people and AI assistants can easily quote, rather than stuffing phrases into paragraphs.

Frequently asked questions

How many keywords should one page target?

One primary topic with a handful of closely related variations. Trying to rank one page for unrelated topics usually weakens it.

Do you write the content?

I can optimize and edit existing content and write detailed briefs. For full copywriting I work with your writers so the voice stays yours.

How long until on-page changes show results?

Search engines need to recrawl and reassess the page, so changes often show in weeks rather than days. Competitive terms take longer, and nobody can promise exact timing.

Is on-page SEO different for landing pages?

The principles are the same, but landing pages also need a focused message, fast loading and clear calls to action so traffic converts.
